What changed and when, newest first. The reference for how things are now is `SPEC.md`; how to work on the code is `CLAUDE.md`.

## 2026-11-26

- **Purpose over chaos: the button's installations on the rooms generator's geometry** (the grandeur design's build order, step 2; SPEC 6.3, 6.4, 9).
  - **`InstallationPlace`** (`generators/installation/place.gd`) lays a written installation on the rooms generator's own geometry: rooms of every size and shape, two and three storeys tall, balconies and ramps, then windows, pools, rooms upstairs and a basement. - **The mission graph: a loop, a gate with two ways past, a shortcut back** (the generation review's build order, step 4 of 4: "generate the logical map first: branches, dependencies, shortcuts"; SPEC 6.3, 6.4, 9).
  - **Links** (a programme's `links`; `ProgrammePlan`, `ProgrammeLayout`). A link joins the far ends of two wings round the outside: both branch corridors run on past everything on their side between them, and a corridor along the next row joins them. So there are two ways between the stretches of spine they leave from. It is a room of the level (`"link": id`), may have a door in its middle, and a lock may lock that door (`{"link": id}`). Its two wings go on one side (one written `"any"` takes the other's side, drawing no number); a side has one link at most.
  - **A release** (`ProgrammeFill`). A flag lock on a spine room may have a `release`: a thing hung on the corridor's wall just outside its door on one side, whose use sets the lock's flag. The lock holds from the other side and lets through from that one.
  - **The proof knows the level's systems** (`ProgrammeCheck`). Each system's state is a key (`sys:power=on`): held from the start, given by a `system` effect, and needed by any door, lift, thing or option with `needs`. Before, the proof took the security panel as usable with the power off. A state once had counts as had for good, the one way the proof is generous. `solve(level, opts)` can `withhold` keys and `seal` cells, to prove a level without one way or another.
  - **The ways through** (`ProgrammeGraph`, `generators/programme/graph.gd`): `loops(level)`, counted on the regions between doors (the doors and links more than a tree of them needs), and `steps(level, from, to, held)`, the fewest steps with those keys.

    - The gate's own way is always on the near side: with security, the far wing is not the security panel's; the generator wing's yellow card, when it has one, is on the near side too.
    - A core whose wings keep no card has its red card in the hub; one draw had none, and its core was left unlocked.

- **Level systems: power, security, air** (the generation review's build order, step 2 of 4: "shared systems, not isolated switches"; SPEC 4.5, 4.10, 4.12, 5.13, 5.25, 7, 9).
  - **`LevelSystems`** (`engine/level/systems.gd`, `Level.systems`). A level's `systems` name which it has, the state each starts in, the rooms it serves and what happens as it comes to a state (`do`). The game's `systems` say what each kind is: HaveNot has
    - `power`: off gives the emergency lights, low and red; or on;
    - `security`: normal, alert (red light) or off;
    - `air`: running, where a cloud clears in 0.4 of the time; or off, where it hangs 1.6 times as long.
  - **What needs them.** A thing with `needs` works only while the systems are so, told now and at every change:
    - a door stays as it is and says why ("No power.");
    - a lift stays put;
    - a station keeps its robot in, whatever wakes it;
    - a trap or a maker is off;
    - a terminal does not answer.
    - A panel option's `needs` is a gate, and its label says what it needs ("[needs Main power ON]").
  - **Changing them.** The `system` effect (`to`, or `next`) changes a state. It tells everything that needs the system, sets the lights of the rooms the system serves, says its line, sounds, emits `system.changed` and runs the level's `do`. The level's memory keeps the states. A terminal's words show `{sys:<name>}`.
  - **Where it hooks in:** `Door`, `Lift` and `Station` each got a `set_working`. `Prop.prompt` and `use` refuse, saying why. `Cloud` lasts by the air. `Spawner` watches what needs a system. `LevelMemory` keeps the states, and `OptionGates` has the `needs` gate.

- **Awareness with memory; the relentless; the startle** (step 4 of 6; Eric, 25 Nov: some monsters "know everything and follow relentlessly", others "move to the last place they saw the player and hunt from there", and "a startle or a jump and turn, moving antenna ... along with an audio cue"; SPEC 3, 5.13, 5.20, 5.24, 9).
  - **Four states** (`ActorAware`, `engine/actor/aware.gd`, on every actor):
    - *Unaware:* asleep.
    - *Alerted:* it heard something and goes to look, at 0.7 of its speed.
    - *Hunting:* it has seen you, been hurt or been told within the last 2.5 s. Only then does it know where you are. In those 2.5 s it follows you only as far as you could have run: round a corner, not up a lift.
    - *Searching:* it lost you. It goes to where it last knew you were and looks round places within 8 m for its `search` seconds (10), or its beat's `give_up`. Then it gives up: back to its beat, or asleep, and for 20 s it sees all round.
  - **The relentless** (`relentless`, a new actor key): the roach, the Glorexx, the Shroud Stalker, the Deadbot, the three Bloodflies, the Garnisher, the Liquidator and the Brood Matron hunt from the moment they wake until they die.
  - **The startle** (`ActorStartle`, `ActorTwitch`). A monster that wakes:
    - turns hard toward what it noticed, and hops (not a flyer, a turret or a thing on a wall);
    - plays its `startle` clip; failing that, twitches its antennae, feelers, stalks, tails, ears, jaws, heads and necks (a skeleton modifier); failing that, jolts;
